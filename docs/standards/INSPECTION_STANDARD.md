# Inspection Standard

**Document ID:** STD-INSP-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Adopted  
**Published:** 2026-08-02

---

# Purpose

This standard establishes a consistent inspection methodology for all properties managed under the Property Operations Guide (POG).

Routine inspections identify maintenance needs early, reduce long-term costs, improve tenant satisfaction, support capital planning, and preserve property value.

---

# Objectives

Property inspections should be:

- Consistent
- Thorough
- Objective
- Documented
- Repeatable
- Actionable

Every inspection should either confirm acceptable condition or generate actionable follow-up items.

---

# Inspection Philosophy

An inspection is an assessment—not a repair.

The purpose is to observe, document, prioritize, and recommend.

Repairs are managed under the Maintenance Standard.

---

# Inspection Types

## Initial Property Baseline

Performed when a property first enters the POG framework.

Purpose:

- Establish baseline condition
- Create equipment inventory
- Record deferred maintenance
- Document major systems

---

## Routine Inspection

Performed on a recurring schedule.

Examples:

- Quarterly
- Semiannual
- Annual

Purpose:

- Verify property condition
- Identify developing issues
- Update maintenance planning

---

## Seasonal Inspection

Performed before seasonal weather changes.

Examples:

- Winter preparation
- Spring inspection
- Summer readiness
- Storm preparation

---

## Tenant Occupancy Inspection

Examples:

- Move-In
- Periodic Occupancy
- Move-Out

These inspections should follow applicable laws and lease requirements.

---

## Post-Event Inspection

Performed after significant events.

Examples:

- Storm damage
- Flood
- Earthquake
- Fire
- Major plumbing leak
- Extended vacancy

---

Every comprehensive inspection should evaluate:

## Exterior

- Roof
- Gutters
- Siding
- Paint
- Windows
- Doors
- Drainage
- Landscaping
- Irrigation
- Fencing
- Driveways
- Walkways

---

## Interior

- Walls
- Ceilings
- Floors
- Doors
- Windows
- Cabinets
- Fixtures
- Smoke/CO Detectors

---

## Mechanical Systems

- HVAC
- Water Heater
- Plumbing
- Electrical
- Appliances

---

## Safety

- Trip hazards
- Water intrusion
- Electrical hazards
- Fire safety
- Security concerns

---

# Inspection Results

Each observation should receive one of the following ratings:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Excellent | No action required |
| Good | Normal wear |
| Fair | Monitor |
| Poor | Maintenance recommended |
| Critical | Immediate action required |

---

# Required Documentation

Every inspection should record:

- Property ID
- Inspection ID
- Date
- Inspector
- Inspection Type
- Weather (Exterior inspections)
- Findings
- Photographs
- Recommended Actions
- Follow-up Required

---

# Follow-Up

Inspection findings should result in one of the following:

- No Action
- Monitor
- Preventive Maintenance
- Corrective Maintenance
- Capital Improvement
- Emergency Repair

---

# Relationship to Other Standards

Inspection findings support:

- Maintenance Standard
- Vendor Management Standard
- Property Lifecycle Planning
- Annual Property Review
- Budget Planning

---

# Guiding Principle

A property should never surprise its owner.

Consistent inspections transform unexpected failures into planned maintenance.
