# Maintenance Standard

**Document ID:** STD-MAINT-001
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Adopted
**Published:** 2026-08-02

---

# Purpose

This standard establishes the maintenance philosophy, scheduling principles, and documentation requirements for all properties managed under the Property Operations Guide (POG).

Proper maintenance preserves property value, reduces lifecycle costs, improves tenant satisfaction, and minimizes unexpected failures.

---

# Objectives

Maintenance activities should be:

- Planned
- Preventive
- Documented
- Repeatable
- Cost effective
- Safety focused

---

# Maintenance Philosophy

POG follows four maintenance categories.

## 1. Preventive Maintenance

Scheduled work intended to prevent failures.

Examples:

- HVAC servicing
- Roof inspections
- Smoke detector testing
- Gutter cleaning
- Water heater flushing

---

## 2. Corrective Maintenance

Repairs resulting from discovered deficiencies.

Examples:

- Plumbing repairs
- Appliance replacement
- Fence repair
- Drywall repair

---

## 3. Predictive Maintenance

Maintenance driven by condition or expected service life.

Examples:

- Roof nearing end of life
- Carpet replacement planning
- Exterior paint lifecycle
- Appliance age monitoring

---

## 4. Emergency Maintenance

Immediate work required to protect life, safety, or property.

Examples:

- Water leaks
- Electrical hazards
- Gas leaks
- Security failures
- Storm damage

---

# Maintenance Priorities

| Priority | Response Goal |
|----------|---------------|
| Emergency | Immediate |
| High | Within 24 hours |
| Medium | Within 7 days |
| Low | Schedule as practical |

---

# Required Maintenance Record

Every maintenance activity should document:

- Property ID
- Date
- Category
- Description
- Vendor (if applicable)
- Cost
- Warranty Information
- Before Photos (optional)
- After Photos (optional)
- Follow-up Required

---

# Preventive Maintenance Schedule

Each property should maintain recurring schedules for:

- HVAC
- Plumbing
- Electrical
- Roof
- Exterior
- Landscaping
- Pest Control
- Smoke/CO Detectors
- Water Heater
- Appliances
- Irrigation
- Pool/Spa (if applicable)

---

# Annual Property Review

Each property should receive a comprehensive annual maintenance review.

The review should include:

- Structural condition
- Mechanical systems
- Safety items
- Cosmetic condition
- Deferred maintenance
- Capital improvement planning

---

# Capital Improvements

Projects extending useful life or increasing value should be tracked separately from routine maintenance.

Examples:

- Roof replacement
- Kitchen remodel
- Flooring replacement
- Solar installation
- HVAC replacement

---

# Documentation

Maintenance history should remain with the property for its entire lifecycle.

Maintenance records belong in the future Private Operations Repository.

# Guiding Principle

The most expensive maintenance is the maintenance that was postponed until failure.

POG emphasizes proactive care over reactive repair.
